% ============================ 1. SUMMARY ==================================
\section{Executive Summary}

This project set out to answer a deceptively simple question in a setting where
the answer can actually be checked: \emph{when a neural network learns physics,
does it rely on the causal drivers of the system, or on spurious correlates?}
It is the ``wolf vs.\ snow'' shortcut-learning problem transplanted into
computational physics --- where synthetic data provides what photographs never
can: a ground-truth answer key, because we decide which factors enter the
governing equations.

\textbf{The project's full synthetic arc is complete.} All five build phases
passed their pre-registered exit gates:

% ============================ 2. PHASES ===================================
\section{Phase-by-Phase Breakdown}

\subsection{Phases 0--1 \textperiodcentered\ Environment, Problem Statement \& Diffusion Warm-up \hfill\passchip{COMPLETE}}
\lab{(a) Objectives}
Reproducible environment; a written problem statement separating the three
claims; de-risk the ML pipeline on the easiest target (2D heterogeneous
diffusion) before the physics gets interesting.
\lab{(b) Deliverables}
Single-script pipeline: py-pde diffusion solver, Gaussian-random-field (GRF)
input sampling, 3{,}000-sample dataset with a held-out correlation-length band,
trained FNO (neuraloperator), evaluation suite, problem-statement document.
\lab{(c) Findings \& Decisions}
GRFs adopted as the principled input distribution (controllable correlation
length and spectral decay $\rightarrow$ clean OOD holdouts). The FNO recipe
(16 modes, 64 hidden channels, 2.4M params) reached 1.23\% ID / 1.20\% OOD
relative L2 --- establishing the architecture reused in every later phase.

\subsection{Phase 2 \textperiodcentered\ Allen--Cahn Simulator \& FNO Surrogate \hfill\passchip{GATES 2A + 2B}}
\lab{(a) Objectives}
A \emph{trustworthy} degradation simulator
($\partial u/\partial t = M(x)\cdot(\varepsilon^2\nabla^2 u + u - u^3 + g)$),
a surrogate accurate enough to stand in for it, and a generator\,$\neq$\,validator
independence check.
\lab{(b) Deliverables}
The modular \code{fnocausal} package replacing the Phase-1 monolith:
config + provenance hashing, per-sample \code{SeedSequence} rng streams, GRFs
and physics metrics; a primary SBDF2 pseudo-spectral solver, an explicit-RK4
validator solver, a py-pde cross-check, and Nyquist-correct Fourier resampling;
an FNO factory and shared training loop; a 6-check verification suite. A
6{,}000-sample dataset (64$\times$64, $T{=}1$) plus a full validator test set
re-solved with the independent scheme at 128$^2$, $\mathrm{d}t/4$. 16 unit tests.
\lab{(c) Findings \& Decisions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scheme choice was measured, not assumed:} first-order semi-implicit
stepping missed the cross-scheme tolerance ($1.8\times10^{-3}$), so the solver
was upgraded to second-order SBDF2 ($7\times10^{-5}$ at $\mathrm{d}t{=}2.5\times10^{-3}$).
Variable mobility uses the standard stabilized splitting (implicit
constant-coefficient part at $M_{\max}$).
\item \textbf{Solver verification is load-bearing:} energy monotonicity, the
shrinking-circle law $\mathrm{d}(R^2)/\mathrm{d}t = -2\varepsilon^2$ (0.8\%
deviation), coarsening scaling, $\mathrm{d}t$/grid convergence, and three-way
scheme agreement all passed before any training data was generated.
\item \textbf{Gate 2b:} ID test 1.52\%; independent-scheme targets 1.53\%
(schemes agree to ${\sim}9\times10^{-4}$ on targets, so the surrogate error
budget is entirely model, not solver); held-out $\varepsilon$-band 1.26\%.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase 3 \textperiodcentered\ Autoencoder Featurization \hfill\passchip{GATE 3 (REVISED)}}
\lab{(a) Objectives}
A compact latent representation $z$ of the material state as the \emph{analysis
substrate} for the causal audit. The FNO always operates on full fields --- the
AE is instrumentation, not pipeline.
\lab{(b) Deliverables}
Convolutional autoencoder (4$\times$ stride-2 encoder, GroupNorm/GELU, mirrored
decoder); a 10-model sweep (2 field variants $\times\ z\in\{8\ldots128\}$);
pixel-reconstruction and functional-fidelity reports with figures.
\lab{(c) Findings \& Decisions}
The planned pixel-L2 gate ($\le 5\%$) is \textbf{informationally impossible}
for saturated two-phase fields: reconstruction error is dominated by interface
placement (rel-L2 $\approx \sqrt{4\delta L}$), so 5\% would demand
${\sim}0.01$-cell interface accuracy from any latent. The gate was re-anchored
on what the audit requires --- preservation of physical observables. At
$z{=}128$: severity $R^2$ 0.975, interface-length $R^2$ 0.932. The pixel curve
remains on the record.

\subsection{Phase 4 \textperiodcentered\ Spurious-Structure Injection --- the Answer Key \hfill\passchip{GATES 4(I)--(IV)}}
\lab{(a) Objectives}
A dataset where a nuisance factor S is \emph{provably} non-causal and a driver
C is \emph{provably} causal, with training environments where S correlates with
the outcome and evaluation environments where the correlation weakens, breaks,
or reverses.
\lab{(b) Deliverables}
8{,}000-simulation pool (nucleation-growth regime, tilted well $g{=}0.3$,
hidden mobility $M(x){=}m\cdot\text{GRF}$); seven environments
(train $\rho\in\{0.95,0.8\}$, control $\rho{=}0$, eval $\rho\in\{0.95,0.5,0,-0.95\}$);
a per-sample answer-key CSV; five trained models --- biased, control, and oracle
FNOs, two severity heads, and the biased-environment AE audited in Phase 5.
\lab{(c) Findings \& Decisions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The load-bearing insight: hide the causal driver.} If a model
sees all causal inputs, the target is deterministic in them and a competent
network ignores any nuisance --- no shortcut, no experiment. With $M(x)$ hidden,
the nuisance genuinely carries information in biased environments, so the
Bayes-optimal predictor \emph{must} use it --- guaranteeing adoption and
guaranteeing collapse under reversal. Exactly the wolf/snow structure.
\item \textbf{Monotonicity by construction, not luck:} traveling-wave analysis
gives front velocity $V = M\varepsilon\,3g/\sqrt{2}$ and critical radius
$R_c = \varepsilon\sqrt{2}/(3g)$ --- \emph{independent of $m$}. Seeding all
nuclei above $R_c$ makes severity monotone in the hidden amplitude for every
sample (confirmed 20/20 in matched-IC interventions; severity spread
0.04--0.88, zero saturation).
\item \textbf{Adoption confirmed (Gate 4iv):} on held-out same-distribution
data, biased beats control (FNO 0.250 vs 0.351 rel-L2; head 0.038 vs 0.083 MAE)
while the oracle sits at 0.0077 --- the full designed ordering.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase 5 \textperiodcentered\ The Causal Audit \hfill\passchip{GATE 5 --- 21/21}}
\lab{(a) Objectives}
Quantitatively separate causal from spurious reliance with multiple
corroborating methods, checking every verdict against the Phase-4 answer key.
\lab{(b) Deliverables}
An audit orchestrator over five analysis modules; a scorecard CSV (every check:
expected pattern $\rightarrow$ measured value $\rightarrow$ verdict); an
auto-generated conclusions document; five figures.
\lab{(c) Findings}
All five diagnostics agree with the answer key (21/21 gate-counted checks):

\toprule
\rowcolor{accentsoft}\textbf{\sffamily\footnotesize Diagnostic} & \textbf{\sffamily\footnotesize Key result}\\
\midrule

Biased head error \textbf{+327\%} under flipped correlation
($0.038\rightarrow0.161$, monotone in $\rho$-shift); control $-4\%$; oracle
flat at 0.0073\\

\medskip
One secondary check produced an honest \textbf{negative result}
\infochip{INFORMATIONAL}: an IRMv1-penalized probe generalized \emph{worse} than
pooled ERM at every penalty weight (0.289 vs 0.425 flipped-environment $R^2$,
$\lambda$ selected on an independent environment). Recorded with its likely
explanation: the pooled baseline is already implicitly invariance-regularized
(half its data comes from the $\rho{=}0$ environment), and linear IRMv1 is
documented to underperform in low-environment-diversity settings
(Rosenfeld et al., 2021).

% ============================ 3. CHALLENGES ===============================
\section{Technical Challenges \& Explanation Report}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0pt,itemsep=8pt,style=unboxed]
\item[]\ctag{C1}\textbf{Long-horizon operator learning is chaotic.}
The first surrogate, trained on a $T{=}4$ horizon, memorized the training set
(train MSE $5.5\times10^{-4}$) yet generalized at 17\% --- best validation at
epoch 6 of 100. A $T{=}4$ one-shot map spans an entire coarsening cascade whose
merger outcomes depend sensitively on the input. \emph{Resolution:} horizon
shortened to $T{=}1$ where the operator is well-conditioned; longer horizons via
autoregressive composition are future work. Documented as a config-level
decision.

\item[]\ctag{C2}\textbf{A hidden-parameter bug that later became the experiment.}
Even at $T{=}1$, 11\% error with instant overfitting. Root cause: the PDE
parameter $\varepsilon$ was not a model input and is not inferable from the
other channels --- the target literally wasn't a function of the inputs.
\emph{Resolution:} $\varepsilon$ fed as a constant channel (standard
parametric-FNO practice) $\rightarrow$ error collapsed 60$\times$ to 1.5\%.
Instructive symmetry: the same hidden-variable structure that was a \emph{bug}
in Phase 2 is deliberately \emph{constructed} in Phase 4, where its early-plateau
training signature reappears by design.

\item[]\ctag{C3}\textbf{Physics checks mis-specified for a finite box.}
The textbook coarsening exponent ($\ell\sim t^{1/2}$) is unmeasurable in a unit
box --- domains reach a quarter of the box within $t\approx1$, leaving no
scaling decade; the structure-factor length is additionally biased by the Porod
tail. \emph{Resolution:} the check was restated in the reachable regime as the
differential form of the same law ($\ell^2$ linear in $\varepsilon^2 t$;
measured $R^2\,0.98$, slope $\approx 9\varepsilon^2$) with an interface-density
length measure.

\item[]\ctag{C4}\textbf{Nyquist handling in spectral resampling.}
Naive Fourier truncation/zero-padding broke the exact up/down-sample round trip
(2.6\% error) by mishandling the ambiguous Nyquist bin. \emph{Resolution:}
Nyquist-splitting resampling matrices (coefficient split on upsample, re-summed
on downsample) --- round trip now exact, validator targets alias-free.

\item[]\ctag{C5}\textbf{The compute wall.}
Single-threaded NumPy solves projected ${\sim}14$\,h for one dataset + validator
set. \emph{Resolution:} float32 CUDA backends for both solvers
(${\sim}100\times$; 25\,min total), with the float64 NumPy path retained as the
verified reference and cross-backend agreement unit-tested ($<10^{-4}$). This
also made the audit's 1{,}280 extra Sobol simulations cheap.

\item[]\ctag{C6}\textbf{Ecosystem breakage.}
SALib 1.5.1 passes its problem names into \code{pd.unique}, which pandas 3.0 no
longer accepts for plain lists. \emph{Resolution:} one-line fix (names as
\code{ndarray}), noted for future SALib use.

\item[]\ctag{C7}\textbf{Two audit checks were initially mis-formalized.}
(i)~Requiring the \emph{multivariate} probe's causal coefficient to be stable
across environments is not what ICP predicts --- with a shortcut available, a
full-representation fit legitimately shifts weight from causal to spurious
directions; that reweighting is itself evidence of adoption. Replaced with
ICP-correct univariate score probes, which pass cleanly; the multivariate
instability is retained as corroborating evidence.
(ii)~The IRM check tested a coefficient ratio rather than IRM's operative
promise (out-of-environment risk); re-formalized with $\lambda$ selected on an
independent shifted environment --- still negative, hence the documented
informational finding. Both re-formalizations are explained inline in the audit
code; \textbf{thresholds were never adjusted to fit results}.
\end{description}

% ============================ 4. VELOCITY / DEBT ==========================
\section{Current Project Velocity \& Technical Debt}

\textbf{Velocity.} Phases 2--5 --- solvers, a ${\sim}30$-module package, 11
trained models, and the five-diagnostic audit --- were delivered in three
working sessions, with all heavy computation fitting a single consumer GPU
(RTX 2070). The exit-gate discipline caught every significant defect
\emph{before} it could contaminate downstream phases.

\textbf{Quality posture.}
\begin{itemize}
\item 16 unit tests (GRF statistics, solver invariants, resampling round trip,
backend agreement, stability guards); all green.
\item Every run is config-driven and seeded; every results CSV carries
\code{run\_id}, \code{config\_hash}, and a \code{claim} tag --- full provenance
for the eventual write-up.
\item Reference-vs-bulk numerical split: float64 NumPy is the verified path;
float32 CUDA is the throughput path, agreement-tested.
\item Per-sample \code{SeedSequence} streams make any sample or intervention
twin exactly regenerable from two integers.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Known technical debt} (ordered by priority):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Single-seed results.} All headline metrics come from one master
seed per phase. Cheap to fix now (GPU backends): a 5-seed replication with
dispersion estimates should precede any publication claim.
\item \textbf{No CI.} Tests and gates run manually; a minimal pipeline
(pytest + ruff on push) would protect the reference solvers from silent
regression.
\item \textbf{Single-direction ablation.} Diagnostic 5 ablates one latent
direction; a small-subspace variant (QR of the top S-responding directions) is
specified in the handoff playbook but not implemented.
\item \textbf{Surrogate horizon.} The Phase-2 surrogate covers $T{=}1$;
autoregressive composition to longer horizons is unvalidated.
\item \textbf{Mitigation is post-hoc only} (latent ablation); training-time
mitigation was probed only via the inconclusive IRM linear probe.
\item Minor: audit script length (${\sim}450$ lines), Windows-specific paths,
${\sim}6$\,GB of duplicated venv/dataset artifacts across the two repos.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================ 5. NEXT STEPS ===============================
\section{Next Steps \& Recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Phase 6a --- Cross-PDE replication (highest priority).} Repeat
Phases 4--5 on Fisher--KPP
($\partial u/\partial t = D\nabla^2 u + r u(1-u)$; traveling fronts, different
dynamical character). A method that only works on Allen--Cahn is overfit to one
equation --- this is the reviewer-facing weak point today, and the
infrastructure was designed for it.
\item \textbf{Phase 6b --- Robustness sweeps.} Re-run the audit under
observation noise, sensor sparsity, and resolution changes; confirm the
verdicts survive realistic measurement conditions.
\item \textbf{Multi-seed replication.} 5 master seeds across Phase 2--5 headline
metrics; report means $\pm$ dispersion ($<1$ day of compute at current
throughput).
\item \textbf{Mitigation experiment.} Compare naive training against an
invariance-trained or ablation-regularized model on the Phase-4 sandbox ---
upgrading the project from \emph{diagnosing} shortcut learning to \emph{fixing}
it. Given the IRM linear-probe result, prioritize representation-level
interventions over IRMv1-style penalties.
\item \textbf{Phase 7 --- Write-up.} Assemble the paper around the three-claims
framing; every figure and CSV already carries provenance tags. The honest IRM
negative result and the two documented check re-formalizations belong in the
paper --- they strengthen, not weaken, the methodology narrative.
\item \textbf{Housekeeping.} Stand up CI, add linting, and write a short
results-directory README mapping each CSV to the claim it supports.
\end{enumerate}
